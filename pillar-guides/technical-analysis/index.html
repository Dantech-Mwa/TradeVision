What Is Technical Analysis?

Technical analysis is the study of historical price action and trading volume to forecast future price movements. It operates on three core principles:

📊 The Three Pillars of Technical Analysis:

1. Price discounts everything — All known information is already reflected in the price.
2. Prices move in trends — Trends persist until they reverse.
3. History repeats itself — Human psychology creates recognizable patterns.

Technical Analysis vs Fundamental Analysis

Factor Technical Analysis Fundamental Analysis
Focus Price & volume patterns Financial health & valuation
Time Horizon Short to medium term Long term
Data Used Charts, indicators, patterns Financial statements, ratios, news
Primary Users Traders, speculators Investors, analysts
Key Tools TradeVision Pro, TradingView Bloomberg, Thomson Reuters
✅ Best Approach: Many successful traders combine both. Use fundamentals to find what to trade and technicals to decide when to trade.

Chart Types & Timeframes

Choosing the right chart type is the first step in technical analysis. Each type presents price data differently, highlighting various aspects of market behavior.

Candlestick Charts

Most popular among traders. Each candle shows four price points: Open, High, Low, Close (OHLC). The body indicates the open-to-close range, while the wicks (shadows) show the high and low.

🕯️ Candlestick Colors:
🟢 Green/White = Bullish (Close > Open)
🔴 Red/Black = Bearish (Close < Open)

Line Charts

Simplest chart type — connects closing prices with a line. Best for identifying overall trends and removing noise from intraday fluctuations.

Bar Charts

Each bar shows OHLC using a vertical line with horizontal tick marks. Provides more detail than line charts while remaining cleaner than candlesticks.

Heikin Ashi

A Japanese charting technique that smoothes price action to filter out noise. Uses modified OHLC values to identify trends more clearly. Excellent for trend-following strategies.

Support & Resistance

Support is a price level where buying interest is strong enough to prevent further declines. Resistance is a level where selling interest prevents further advances. Together, they form the foundation of technical analysis.

📌 Key Concepts:

• Support becomes resistance when broken (and vice versa)
• Stronger levels = More touches / Longer history
• Round numbers often act as psychological support/resistance
• Volume confirms the strength of these levels

How to Identify Support & Resistance

  1. Historical swing highs and lows — Previous reaction points
  2. Moving averages — Dynamic support/resistance
  3. Trendlines — Diagonal support/resistance
  4. Fibonacci levels — Mathematical support/resistance
  5. Volume Profile — High-volume nodes act as support/resistance

Fibonacci Tools

Leonardo Fibonacci discovered a mathematical sequence that appears throughout nature — and in financial markets. The key ratios are 23.6%, 38.2%, 50%, 61.8%, and 78.6%.

📐 Fibonacci Retracement Levels:

0.618 (61.8%) — Golden Ratio. Most important level.
0.382 (38.2%) — Secondary support/resistance.
0.500 (50%) — Psychological level.
0.786 (78.6%) — Deep retracement level.

How to use: Draw from swing high to swing low (or vice versa). Watch for price reactions at each Fibonacci level. Combine with candlestick patterns for higher probability trades.

Elliott Wave Theory

Developed by Ralph Nelson Elliott in the 1930s, this theory suggests that markets move in predictable wave patterns driven by investor psychology.

🌊 Elliott Wave Structure:

Impulse Waves (5 waves): Waves 1, 3, 5 move with the trend. Waves 2, 4 are corrections.
Corrective Waves (3 waves): Waves A, B, C move against the trend.

Market Structure

Institutional traders use market structure to identify high-probability setups. Key concepts include:

  • Order Blocks (OB) — Large institutional orders that act as support/resistance
  • Fair Value Gaps (FVG) — Price inefficiencies that often get filled
  • Liquidity Sweeps — Stop-hunting behavior by institutions
  • Break of Structure (BOS) — Trend confirmation
  • Change of Character (ChoCh) — Potential trend reversal

Risk Management

Risk management is the most important skill in trading. Even the best strategy will fail without proper risk management.

The 1% Rule

Never risk more than 1-2% of your account on a single trade. This ensures no single loss can wipe out your account.

Account Size 1% Risk 2% Risk

$5,000$50$100

Position Sizing Formula

📐 Position Size = (Account Risk) ÷ (Stop Loss Distance)

Example: $10,000 account, risking 1% ($100), stop loss at $100 below entry.
Position Size = $100 ÷ $100 = 1 unit (e.g., 1 BTC)

Risk/Reward Ratio

Only take trades with a positive risk/reward ratio. A 1:2 risk/reward means you risk $100 to make $200. Even with a 50% win rate, you're profitable.

✅ Golden Rule: Always know your exit before you enter. Set both Stop Loss and Take Profit before entering any trade.

Building Your Trading System

A trading system is a complete set of rules for entering, managing, and exiting trades. Here's a step-by-step framework:

  1. Define Your Edge — What makes your strategy work? (e.g., divergence, pattern recognition, trend following)
  2. Define Entry Rules — Be specific. "Buy when RSI < 30 and price bounces from support."
  3. Define Exit Rules — When to take profit? When to cut losses?
  4. Define Position Size — How much to risk per trade?
  5. Backtest — Test on historical data before live trading.
  6. Forward Test — Demo trade for 1-3 months.
  7. Optimize — Adjust parameters based on results.
  8. Execute — Go live with real money.
